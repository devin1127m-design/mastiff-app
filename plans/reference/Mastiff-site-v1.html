01 · Setup · day one

Load the vendor list, set the rules

Thirty minutes with your bookkeeper: every vendor’s name, bank, last four and the number you already call back. Then the owner sets three numbers: the single-payment line, your payment hours, and who can be the second person on a release.

Nothing installed. No access to your bank or your books.
02 · The wall · day one onward

Every payment request gets checked before it is keyed

Your bookkeeper enters the payee, the amount and the account. Mastiff answers Pass or Hold with the reason: a payee not on file, a change of bank details, an amount out of range, a request that arrived at 9 PM on a Saturday, or one that pushes to hurry. A hold ends only when someone calls the number on file and logs the call with a second person’s name. Then the bank-side controls, dual release and payee limits, are set with your banker in the room.

Pass or Hold, like an error code. Not a judgment call made alone at 4:55 PM.

Once a week for thirty days we sit with the log: what held, what was released, what got rejected, how long holds took. On day 30 you get a one-page read-out of what the wall caught and what it cost you in time. You keep the log either way.

No contract. Stop any day with one message.
The three rules

Every case we have studied was lost by breaking one of these.

Scams change costume. The tells do not: money going to an account you have never paid, an urgent request outside the normal pattern, a person skipping a callback.

  • 01
    The callback rule.Bank details change only after a call to a number already on file, made by someone other than the person who received the request. The payment holds until the call is logged.
  • 02
    The cap.A dollar amount above which no single person can send, and a weekly total, written down and enforced at the bank with dual release.
  • 03
    The urgency rule.Any request to skip the process is the reason to slow down. The owner commits in writing to never be angry about a confirmation call.
The difference

You already pay for three kinds of protection. None of them checks the payment.

Keep all three. Mastiff sits in the gap between them.

Your IT company

Protects the inbox

Filters the junk, runs phishing training, locks email down with MFA. Good, and the best fakes still get through, because they look exactly like your real vendor.

Misses: the request that arrives looking legitimate.
Your bank

Confirms it was you

Positive pay stops checks and debits you never authorized. The approve-wire step confirms you meant to send it.

Misses: a wire you approved to the wrong account. That is how every loss happens.
Your AP software

Emails the vendor to confirm

BILL and tools like it email the vendor when bank details change, to the same inbox a thief may already control, and let you pay before anyone answers.

Misses: a call to the number you already had.
Mastiff

Checks the payment itself

Every payee, amount and bank change is checked against what is on file before it is keyed. A hold clears only after a call to the number on file and a second person’s sign-off. Every check and every call is written down, so when your insurer or your CPA asks how you knew a payment was real, you hand them the log.
