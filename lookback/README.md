# Mastiff look-back scan

Standalone, standard-library-only Python 3.12 tool. A CSV of an office's past payments goes in; a Look-back report of every payment that would have held under Mastiff's rules comes out (CSV and a print-ready HTML page). Spec: PLAN-2.md, Step 1b. Mastiff flags. Your firm makes every payment decision.

## Run

From the repo root:

    python -m lookback preview payments.csv
    python -m lookback scan payments.csv --out reports/acme-lookback --org "Acme Builders"
    python -m lookback scan payments.csv --bills bills.csv --line 5000 --out reports/acme-lookback

`scan` prints the summary line and writes `<out>.csv`, `<out>.html` and `<out>-money.csv`. `preview` shows how each column was matched and the row counts; run it first and confirm the matching with the office. A bad file gives a plain message on stderr and exit code 2.

Payments CSV: `date`, `payee`, `amount` required; `method`, `check_or_ref`, `invoice_no`, `memo`, `account_last4`, `routing_last4` optional. Headers are matched loosely (Vendor, Paid To, Amt, Check #, Acct...). Dates in common US formats; amounts like `$1,234.56` or `(1,234.56)`. Optional bills CSV: `payee`, `amount`, `invoice_no`, `date`, plus `terms` (or `discount_date` and `discount_percent`) for the discount check.

## Data rule

Nothing longer than 4 digits of an account or tax ID is kept. On import, account columns are cut to the last 4 digits and any run of 8 or more digits (or 5 or more after words like acct, routing, EIN) in any text field becomes `****1234`. The original file is never modified, and no longer number is ever written to a report.

## Rules (weight order)

| # | Rule | Verdict word |
|---|------|--------------|
| 1 | Same vendor, different account (last 4 or routing changed) | would have held |
| 2 | Paid once and never again, if a round amount or over the line | would have held |
| 3 | Duplicate: same vendor and amount within 14 days, or same invoice number twice | worth checking |
| 4 | Lookalike vendor names (similarity 0.88+) | worth checking |
| 5 | Over 2x the vendor's trailing average (prior payments only, 3+ prior), or over the line with no history | would have held |
| 6 | 3+ payments to one payee within 30 days, each within 5% under the line | worth checking |
| 7 | No matching bill (only when a bills file is loaded) | worth checking |

Rules 1, 2 and 5 are the three live holds (bank details changed, payee not on file, amount out of range). The rest are cautions. Two established vendors (6+ payments each) with different accounts are not treated as lookalikes. All thresholds live in the `Settings` dataclass in `rules.py`.

`rules.scan(payments, settings, bills=None)` is a pure function: no I/O, no clock, inputs not mutated.

## Report

Summary line: `700 payments, 18 months, 3 would have held, 2 need a call this week`. "Need a call" counts payments flagged by rule 1 or 2 in the most recent 30 days of data; if none, the top 3 by weight. The table is sorted by weight then date, with an empty Status column for the office (to verify / verified fine / confirmed loss). The HTML is self-contained, prints on Letter, and uses black, white and #6B6B6B.

## Tests

    python -m unittest discover -s lookback/tests -t .

The 700-row fixture (`tests/fixtures/payments_700.csv`, plus `bills_700.csv`) is generated by `python -m lookback.tests.fixtures.make_fixture` (seeded, deterministic). It plants one pattern per rule 1-7, the money findings (two more duplicate pairs, four late-fee rows, a lender row that must not flag, three missed and two in-window discounts) and two clean look-alike pairs (Oakland Electric / Oakland Electrical Supply, Brandon Tile / Brandon Tiles) that must not flag. The sample outputs in `samples/` come from it.

## Known limits

- Masked long invoice numbers (8+ digits) keep only their last 4 digits, so two long invoice numbers ending alike could look like a repeat.
- Rule 2 cannot know a recent one-off payee will not be paid again; it says so in context and leaves the call to the office.

## Day-30 read-out

    python -m lookback readout log.csv --out reports/acme-day30 --org "Acme Builders" [--line 5000] [--start 2026-09-01 --end 2026-09-30]

Reads the Wall log export and writes `<out>.html` (one Letter page), `<out>.csv` (the question table) and `<out>-holds.csv` (every hold row). Account values longer than 4 digits are cut on import. Closed holds are released, rejected or override; void entries are counted separately. The page shows questions carriers commonly ask on applications, from public forms, beside the office's own record. It is not coverage advice.


## Money worth checking

`lookback/money.py`: `find_money(payments, bills, settings)` is a pure function. `scan` adds a second sentence to the summary line when any total is non-zero ("Possible money back or stopped: $A in duplicate payments, $B in late fees and interest, $C in missed discounts."), a "Money worth checking" section in the HTML (after the summary, before the holds table) and `<out>-money.csv` (finding, vendor, date(s), amount, what to check). `<out>.csv` is unchanged.

1. Possible duplicate payments: rule 3's detection; the later payment of each pair is the amount to ask back.
2. Late fees and interest paid: whole-word, case-insensitive match on payee, memo and invoice fields (late fee/charge/payment, finance or interest charge, penalty, NSF, returned payment fee, past due fee, reinstatement fee, or a memo of just "LATE"). A lender whose memo says "loan payment" or "mortgage" is skipped for interest wording, but an explicit fee there still counts. Every row reads "worth checking".
3. Early-payment discounts missed: only with a bills file that has a terms column (terms, payment terms, discount terms) or discount date and percent columns. Reads "2/10 net 30", "2% 10 net 30", "1/15 n45"; a bill paid after the window closed counts the discount as missed. Terms are never guessed; otherwise the section says "Not checked: the bills file has no payment terms."

## 1099 readiness

    python -m lookback 1099 payments.csv --vendors vendors.csv --org "<Office>"

Lists vendors at or near the 1099 line for the tax year ($600 for payments through 2025, $2,000 from 2026; inflation-adjusted from 2027, pass `--threshold`), with W-9 status and what to do. Card and payment-app payments are left out (1099-K); goods-only vendors do not need a 1099-NEC. The vendor CSV is optional: vendor, W-9 on file (yes/no), entity type, TIN last 4. Advisory: the accountant confirms every form.

## Subcontractor check (construction)

    python -m lookback subs --template register.csv      # blank register for the office
    python -m lookback subs register.csv --payments payments.csv --org "<Office>"

The register lists each sub with GL, workers' comp and auto expiry dates, additional insured (yes/no) and the date the last lien waiver covers through. Findings are cautions only: lapsed or expiring certificates, payments made while a certificate had lapsed, and payments not covered by a waiver. Wording: "Mastiff flags. It does not give legal advice."
