Payment controls for small offices

Every payment request gets checked before it gets paid.

Most losses do not begin with a hacked bank account. They begin with a request: a vendor's "new bank details," an invoice that looks right, a call that sounds like the owner. AI has made these requests harder to spot. Mastiff checks each payment request against what your office already has on file before anyone keys it. When a request does not match, it is held, and we call the vendor at the number on file on your behalf.

The problem

Payment requests are where small offices lose money.

Altered bank details and urgent payment requests reach small offices every day. The figures below are drawn from published sources.

  • 81%

    In an August 2026 survey of 230 senior finance executives at large organizations, 81% said their company had faced an AI-enabled fraud attempt in the past year. Only 50% were very or extremely confident they could spot one before money moved.1

  • $3.05 billion

    The FBI logged $3.05 billion in business email compromise losses in 2025. It was the second-largest category by reported loss, after investment fraud.2

  • $93,425

    A small construction firm lost $93,425 when a subcontractor's bank details were changed without the callback its own policy required (2019 claims case study).3

  • Check your policy

    Ask your broker what your cyber policy says about callbacks or second confirmation for payment changes. Wording varies by policy.4

What you get

Mastiff does the verification work. Your office makes the decisions.

  1. 01 The check

    Every payment request is reviewed by Mastiff before it is keyed. You receive Pass or Hold, with the reason stated in one line.

  2. 02 We make the calls

    On every hold, we call the vendor at the number already on file and report the result within one hour, Monday through Friday, 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. Your designated second approver signs off. Your bookkeeper is never required to make the call.

  3. 03 Your verified vendor file

    Every call is recorded against the vendor. By day 30, your office holds a vendor file in which each active vendor's phone number and account have been confirmed by voice, dated, and attributed to the person who confirmed them.

  4. 04 New vendor onboarding

    For each new subcontractor or supplier, we collect the W-9, match the legal name, and confirm the account with the bank before the first payment. Your office records only the last four digits.

  5. 05 If a payment goes wrong

    A one-page checklist for the first 24 hours: bank recall request, FBI IC3 report and notice to your insurer, along with the log to provide them. We do not guarantee recovery.

On day 30, you receive a renewal packet: your payment record mapped to the questions insurance carriers ask, ready to share with your broker.

Who it is for

Offices with a bookkeeper and a checkbook.

Mastiff is built for small offices that pay vendors by wire, ACH or check. We are starting with general contractors and subcontractors in Oakland County, Michigan, followed by accounting and advisory practices. If one person keys payments and another owns the money, Mastiff is designed for you.

Three rules

Every case we reviewed came down to one of these.

The method varies. The warning signs do not: money going to an account you have never paid, an urgent request outside the normal pattern, or a skipped callback.

  1. The callback rule.Bank details change only after a call to a number already on file. Someone other than the person who received the request makes the call. The payment waits until the call is logged.
  2. The cap.Set a dollar limit above which no single person can release a payment, plus a weekly total. Put it in writing and enforce it at the bank with dual approval.
  3. The urgency rule.A request to skip the process is itself a reason to slow down. The owner commits in writing that no one will be faulted for making a confirmation call.

The difference

You already pay for three kinds of protection. None of them checks the payment.

Keep all three. Mastiff sits in the gap between them.

  • Your IT company

    Protects the inbox

    Filters spam, runs phishing training and requires multi-factor authentication. All worthwhile. The most convincing requests still get through, because they appear to come from your real vendor.

    Misses: the request that arrives looking legitimate.

  • Your bank

    Confirms it was you

    Positive pay stops checks and debits you did not authorize, and wire approval confirms that you intended to send the payment.

    Misses: a wire you approved yourself to the wrong account.

  • Your AP software

    Emails the vendor

    BILL's help site says it emails the vendor when bank details change. It also says the confirmation "isn't required to schedule payments."5 In the tools we reviewed, a bank-detail change is flagged or confirmed by email. We found none that holds the payment until someone calls the number on file.6

    Misses: a call to the number you already had.

  • Mastiff

    Checks the payment itself

    Every payee, amount and bank change is checked against what is on file before it is keyed. We call the vendor at the number on file, and a second person at your office signs off. Every check and every call is documented. When your insurer or CPA asks how a payment was verified, you hand them the log.

    Enterprise tools are sold by quote only. Mastiff is built for offices with one bookkeeper and a checkbook.
