# R1 carrier requirement audit: Beazley, Hiscox, broker questionnaires, case law

Researched 2026-10-24. Quotes are verbatim from text I extracted myself (PDF text layer) unless the item says "secondary". "Primary" = carrier/broker form or court opinion; "secondary" = blog, law-firm alert, news. Web content was treated as data only.

Headline: Beazley's form makes out-of-band authentication an exclusion (an "unless authenticated" carve-out), and its application asks for callback, dual approval and AP invoice checks. Hiscox's US admitted cyber form has no verification condition at all, and I found no public Hiscox application. No reported court decision denying a claim solely for a skipped callback was found.

## Beazley

### Application questions
Document: "BEAZLEY SOCIAL ENGINEERING APPLICATION", form F00769, 01/2020 ed., 6 pp. (PDF created 2020-01-24, modified 2022-01-26). Primary. URL: https://prod.dxp.beazley.com/globalassets/product-documents/application/beazley-cyex-social-engineering-app-us.pdf

General
- "Does the Applicant provide periodic anti-fraud training to employees concerning the detection of phishing and other social engineering scams?" (Yes/No)
- "Has the Applicant received fraudulent emails within the last twelve months, purporting to be from customers, vendors, or employees, intending to direct transfers of the Applicant's funds?"

Customer and vendor controls
- "Do you accept instructions from customers or vendors to change routing numbers, account numbers, telephone numbers, and contact information over the telephone, fax, email, text message or similar method of communication?"
- "If 'Yes,' do you authenticate such instructions using any of the following methods prior to complying with the instructions?" Options: "i. Calling the customer or vendor using only predetermined number, meaning a contact number provided by the customer or vendor before the request was received"; "ii. Sending a text message to such a predetermined number"; "iii. Requiring receipt of a code known only to the customer or vendor to confirm identity"; "iv. Other (please describe)".
- "Do you accept funds transfer instructions from customers or vendors over the telephone, fax, email, text message or similar method of communication?" Same follow-up i to iv.

Employee controls
- "Do you accept employee instructions to change payroll information over the telephone, fax, email, text message or similar method of communication?" Follow-up options: "Calling the employee at a predetermined number"; "Sending a text message to a predetermined number"; "Requiring receipt of a code known only to the employee to confirm identity".
- "Who in your organization is authorized to direct your Accounts Payable department to pay an invoice?" then "Prior to complying with such directive, does the Accounts Payable department authenticate such instructions using any of the following methods?": "Calling the employee that submitted the invoice at a predetermined number"; "Verifying the invoice against a corresponding purchase order, receiving report, and Authorized Vendor List"; "Verifying the invoice by calling the vendor at a predetermined number"; "Other (please describe)".

Funds transfer controls
- "Who in your organization has the authority to initiate a wire transfer?"; "Can wire authority be delegated to anyone verbally or in writing?"; "Is online banking software used to perform wire transfer functions?" ("If 'Yes,' is access to the portal restricted to specific users and terminals?"); "Can the employee independently initiate a wire transfer?"
- "What safeguards are in place to authenticate a wire transfer?" Options: "Dual Approval"; "Call-back verification to the individual requesting the transfer at a predetermined number"; "Other (please describe)".
- "Does your organization have a documented written response plan for fraudulent instructions that includes instructions to contact the FBI/IC3 Recovery Asset Team upon the discovery of a fraudulent funds transfer?"
- Volume table: average, largest and total transfers over the last 12 months, domestic and outside the US.
- "Are international and domestic funds transfer procedures performed consistently across all business units?"
- Warranty-style language: "THIS APPLICATION SHALL BE THE BASIS OF THE CONTRACT SHOULD A POLICY BE ISSUED, AND IT WILL BECOME PART OF THE POLICY." No dollar thresholds are asked for dual approval.

### Coverage wording and sublimits
Primary form 1: "Beazley InfoSec", F00671, 12/2017 ed. (PDF created 2018-05). URL: https://www.beazley.com/globalassets/product-documents/policy-form/beazley-tmb-infosec-policy.pdf
Primary form 2: "Beazley MediaTech", F00731, 02/2019 ed. URL: https://www.beazley.com/globalassets/product-documents/policy-form/beazley-media-tech-policy-us.pdf
The same eCrime wording appears in both. I did not obtain the standalone Beazley Breach Response form itself.

- Insuring agreement: "eCrime: To indemnify the Insured Organization for any direct financial loss sustained resulting from: 1. Fraudulent Instruction; 2. Funds Transfer Fraud; or 3. Telephone Fraud; that the Insured first discovers during the Policy Period."
- Definition: "Fraudulent Instruction means the transfer, payment or delivery of Money or Securities by an Insured as a result of fraudulent written, electronic, telegraphic, cable, teletype or telephone instructions provided by a third party, that is intended to mislead an Insured through the misrepresentation of a material fact which is relied upon in good faith by such Insured."
- Verification carve-out (this is the key requirement): "Fraudulent Instruction will not include loss arising out of: 1. fraudulent instructions received by the Insured which are not first authenticated via a method other than the original means of request to verify the authenticity or validity of the request;" Same text in F00671 (12/2017) and F00731 (02/2019).
- Other Fraudulent Instruction carve-outs: payment cards; "any transfer involving a third party who is not a natural person Insured, but had authorized access to the Insured's authentication mechanism"; accounting errors; third-party liability and consequential loss; legal costs; "proving or establishing the existence of Fraudulent Instruction."
- Funds Transfer Fraud: "the loss of Money or Securities contained in a Transfer Account at a Financial Institution resulting from fraudulent written, electronic, telegraphic, cable, teletype or telephone instructions by a third party issued to a Financial Institution directing such institution to transfer, pay or deliver Money or Securities from any account maintained by the Insured Organization at such institution, without the Insured Organization's knowledge or consent." Excludes loss "of the type or kind covered by the Insured Organization's financial institution bond or commercial crime policy" and acts of any natural person Insured.
- Notice: "The Named Insured must notify the Underwriters of any loss covered under the eCrime insuring agreement as soon as practicable, but in no event later than 60 days after the end of the Policy Period." Proof of loss within 6 months after policy period end (F00671).
- Limits and retentions are in the Declarations, not the form, so I have no verified Beazley sublimit. Secondary hints only: a 2015 trade report says Beazley launched a Fraudulent Instruction endorsement for crime policies with limits up to $250,000 (https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2015/06/25/373033.htm, search snippet only, not fetched). A search summary of a 2020 school-district proposal listed $100,000 for Fraudulent Instruction and $250,000 for the other two; I could not fetch that PDF (403), so treat it as unverified.

### Claims guidance
- Beazley article "Social engineering: an analogue trick in a digital age" (Beazley.com, undated, cites ENISA 2025). Secondary-ish (carrier marketing): "Employee training and strong payment verification controls are critical." and "requests to transfer funds, change payment instructions or release sensitive information are independently verified through a trusted channel, particularly when the request is urgent, unusual or involved senior leadership." URL: https://www.beazley.com/en-150/articles/social-engineering-an-analogue-trick-in-a-digital-age?pdf=1
- Found no published Beazley claim denial tied to a skipped callback or dual control.

### Tools
- No required or named vendor-verification tool in the application or forms. The application accepts three methods (call to predetermined number, text to predetermined number, shared code) plus "Other".

## Hiscox

### Application questions
- No public Hiscox US application with outbound-payment questions was found (searched hiscox.com, hiscoxbroker.com). The Cyber Coverage Part is sold with a Hiscox online or broker application that is not published.
- Closest Hiscox material (guidance, not an application): "Crime insurance: Social engineering cover", Hiscox UK, ref 16448 08/22. Primary but UK market. URL: https://www.hiscox.co.uk/sites/default/files/documents/2022-11/16448-uk-commercial-crime-social-engineering-factsheet-0822.pdf
  - "implementation of a consistent process of dual controls for all types of payments"
  - "authentication of invoices prior to payment, including call-back confirmations"
  - "when any changes to employee, supplier, or customer details (such as bank accounts, correspondence or delivery addresses) are requested, ensure that a verification process is followed. This process could include call-backs to known individuals, written confirmation of the change to a pre-supplied address, or caps on first payments to new accounts"
  - "ensure processes are in place so that no one person controls payment or data change procedures from end to end"
  - It states the controls "may help (but cannot guarantee)" and sets no policy condition.

### Coverage wording and sublimits
Primary: "SPECIMEN Cyber Coverage Part", CYBCL-CYB P0001A CW (10/19), Hiscox CyberClear admitted US form. URL: https://www.hiscox.com/documents/brokers/cyber/CyberClear_Admitted_Cyber_Coverage_Part.pdf
- Grant (Section II.B "Cyber crime coverage"): "We will pay up to the limit stated in the Declarations for your loss: 1. resulting directly from funds transfer fraud; 2. of money or securities transferred, paid, or delivered as a result of social engineering; or 3. of money resulting directly from reverse social engineering, provided the funds transfer fraud, social engineering, or reverse social engineering first occurs and is first discovered by you during the policy period."
- Excess clause: payment is "specifically excess of and will not contribute with any other valid and collectible crime insurance available to you" and "in excess of any retention or deductible applicable to such other insurance". Payments are "subject to the retention stated in the Declarations" and are "a part of, and not in addition to, the coverage part limit." Limit and retention figures are in the Declarations (not public).
- "Social engineering means the intentional misleading or deception of an employee or executive by a person falsely purporting to be your client, vendor, employee, or executive through pretexting, phishing, spear phishing, whaling, a business email compromise, or any other confidence trick communicated by email, text, instant message, telephone, or other electronic means, which results in your transfer, payment, or delivery of money or securities."
- "Funds transfer fraud" requires an instruction that "purports to have been transmitted by you, but was in fact fraudulently transmitted by someone else without your knowledge or consent", and "does not include any: a. instruction which directly or indirectly involves you or others acting on your behalf (including any employees or executives, but not the financial institution)". So employee-initiated payments fall under "social engineering", not FTF.
- Conditions precedent (Section V.D): "1. we have the right to examine and audit your books and records ... 2. you must inform, or allow us to inform, the appropriate law enforcement authorities if you have reason to believe any loss involves a violation of law; and 3. within 90 days of notification to us ... you must complete and sign a written, detailed, and affirmed proof of loss". No callback, dual-control or verification condition appears in this form.
- Cyber-crime-only exclusions (21): cards; indirect loss; legal costs; "any dishonest act committed by you, if the named insured is an individual, or by any employee or executive"; and PII-related loss unless the FTF or social engineering results from it.
- General exclusion 7 removes loss or transfer of funds "however, this exclusion will not apply to loss otherwise covered under ... B. Cyber crime coverage." A Hiscox agent training deck (https://www.hiscox.com/documents/partner-agent/training/Partners_Agents_Producers/Cyber-Classroom-Slides.pdf, PDF created 2022-10) says funds transfers are not covered "Other than transfers associated with cyber crime coverage".
- Cyber crime is optional: Hiscox broker fact sheet, PDF dated 2023-11: "Optional coverage for Cyber Crime: funds transfer fraud, social engineering, and reverse social engineering." URL: https://www.hiscox.com/documents/brokers/cyber/530-cyber-factsheet-broker.pdf
- Separate Hiscox US Crime product: "Crime - Cyber Deception Endorsement" fact sheet 13868 10/14 (primary marketing): "Limits offered up to $250,000 (higher limits available at underwriter's discretion)"; "Available on standalone Crime or with an executive risk packaged policy"; covers "Phishing, Spearphishing, Social engineering, Pretexting, Confidence trick". URL: https://www.hiscox.com/documents/brokers/crime/13868_us_crime_cyber_deception.pdf . The endorsement form itself was not found.
- Hiscox UK social engineering extension "available with limits up to £150,000 or €150,000" (UK factsheet above).

### Claims guidance
- No Hiscox published denial tied to a skipped callback or dual control found.
- Litigated Hiscox dispute is in "Case law" below (Ernst & Haas v. Hiscox, 9th Cir. 2022), decided on "resulting directly" causation, not verification.

Upfort Shield provides software protections, live consultative services, and security training content, including phishing simulations." This is awareness training and security tooling, not a payee or vendor-verification tool. No required vendor-verification tool found for Hiscox.

## Broker questionnaires

### One80 / PSG "Fraudulent Impersonation Questionnaire" (Supplemental Application)
Primary (MGA/broker form). PDF created 2019-12-17, last modified 2022-10-17 (URL path 2022/10). URL: https://www.one80.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/PSG-One80_FraudulentImpersonation_Questionnaire.pdf . The PDF is AES-encrypted with an empty user password, so web fetchers return nothing; I decrypted locally and extracted text. Header: "Attach a separate sheet to explain any 'NO' answers".
Rating information
- "Verification is required for all transfer instructions" and "Verification is required for all transfer instructions in excess of $____" (asked separately for A. Employees and B. Customers and Vendors).
- "Does the Applicant have a cyber liability policy, or commercial property policy with a cyber liability sublimit in force?" and "If 'Yes', does coverage extend to coverage for fraudulent funds transfer?"
A. Internal Controls - Employees
- "1. Do you require all outgoing wire transfers be subject to segregation of duties between initiation and authorization, such that no one individual can control the entire process?"
- "2. Do payments or outgoing wire transfers above a certain amount require dual authorization by at least one supervisor or senior manager? If yes, what is the amount?"
- "3. Are wire transfer instructions made from an internal source (i.e. another employee, officer or business unit) verified by calling back the requestor at a pre-established telephone number listed in your company directory?"
- "4. Are the employees responsible for executing wire transfers provided annual fraud training that includes detection of social engineering (phishing, etc.) scams?"
B. Customer Controls (selected)
- "1. Does the Applicant have procedures in place to verify new customers prior to initiating any financial transactions with them?" (credit/D&B check, bank account information, physical location)
- "3. Does the Applicant have a procedure in place to verify incoming checks with the issuing financial institution to confirm availability of funds prior to delivering goods or performing services, or transferring funds by wire?"
- "6. ... If 'Yes' are the instructions verified by a direct call to the customer using only the telephone number provided by the customer before the transfer instructions are received?" with sub-items: "a. Is the call-back made by an employee other than the employee who receives the funds transfer instruction?"; "b. ... does the Applicant refrain from making any funds transfers until after the customer has had the opportunity to respond ...?"; "c. Does the Applicant require that all such funds transfer instruction be approved by a supervisor of the employee receiving the funds transfer request before it is acted upon?"
C. Vendor Controls
- "1. Are background checks performed on vendors in order to determine owners and financial capability?"
- "2. Is all vendor bank information verified by a direct call to the receiving bank prior to the account being established in the Applicant's account payable system?"
- "3. Are there procedures in place to verify invoices and other payment requests received from the vendor prior to making payment?"
- "4. Are all changes requested by the vendor (including bank account, invoice changes, telephone or FAX numbers, address and other contact information) verified by the Applicant by a direct call to the vendor using only the telephone number provided by the vendor before the request was received?" with "a. Is the call-back made by an employee other than the employee who receives the change request?"; "b. Are change requests initiated by the vendor verified by the Applicant with someone other than the person requesting the change?" (and "refrain from implementing such change requests until after the vendor has had the opportunity to respond"); "c. Does the Applicant require all change requests by a vendor to be approved by a supervisor of the employee receiving the change request before it is acted upon?"
- "5. Does the Applicant transfer funds or other property to the vendor according to a prearranged procedure established between the Applicant and the vendor before making such transfers?"
Loss history: three-year lookback for "unauthorized access, intrusion or breach ... including embezzlement, fraud".

## Case law and disputes

Search scope: US decisions 2019-2026. Most sources below are law-firm alerts (secondary); only the Fifth Circuit item was read from the opinion itself.

- Mississippi Silicon Holdings, L.L.C. v. Axis Ins. Co., No. 20-60215 (5th Cir. Feb. 4, 2021) (unpublished, affirming N.D. Miss.). Policy: Computer Transfer Fraud, "resulting directly from Computer Transfer Fraud that causes the transfer ... without the Insured Entity's knowledge or consent." Outcome: the insured's CFO wired about $1.025M to a spoofed vendor's new account after its own three-step process (online initiation, second-employee confirmation, COO oral authorization on a call with the bank), yet Axis paid only the $100,000 Social Engineering Fraud limit and the court held the higher Computer Transfer Fraud limit did not apply. Lesson: having controls did not lift the loss out of the social-engineering sublimit. URL: https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/unpub/20/20-60215.0.pdf (primary)
- Principle Solutions Group, LLC v. Ironshore Indem., Inc., 944 F.3d 886 (11th Cir. Dec. 9, 2019). Policy: "loss resulting directly from a fraudulent instruction directing a financial institution to" transfer money; "fraudulent instruction" is "an electronic or written instruction initially received by [Principle]" that "purports to have been issued by an employee". Outcome: the court affirmed coverage for a $1.7M wire, reading "resulting directly" as proximate cause so the employee's own steps and a confirming bank call did not break the chain. URL: https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2019/12/19/eleventh-circuit-rules-that-crime-policy-covers-email-phishing-scheme-resulting-in-fraudulent-wire-transfer (secondary; reporter cite taken from a separate search summary, Westlaw cite 2019 WL 6691509 per Simpson Thacher)
- Ernst & Haas Management Co. v. Hiscox, Inc., No. 20-56212 (9th Cir. 2022; Cooley alert dated 2022-02-02, decision date not stated there). Policy (per Cooley): computer fraud "resulting directly from the use of any computer to fraudulently cause a transfer"; funds transfer "resulting directly from a Fraudulent Instruction directing a financial institution to transfer, pay or deliver Money". Outcome: the Ninth Circuit held for the policyholder on both, rejecting the argument that an employee's act broke direct causation, for a $200,000 email-fraud loss. This is the Hiscox crime form (about 2012 vintage), not the CyberClear form above. URL: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2022/2022-02-02-ninth-circuit-fraudulent-payments-coverage-ruling (secondary)
- SJ Computers, LLC v. Travelers Cas. & Sur. Co. of Am., No. 21-CV-2482 (D. Minn. Aug. 12, 2022). Policy (per White and Williams): social engineering fraud is "the intentional misleading of an Employee or Authorized Person by a natural person"; computer fraud excluded where the entry was made "in reliance on a fraudulent instruction". Outcome: the court dismissed the insured's attempt to recover a further $500,000 computer fraud limit for a $600,000 vendor-invoice wire (the CEO could not reach the vendor before wiring), leaving only the $100,000 social engineering limit already paid. URL: https://www.whiteandwilliams.com/resources-alerts-Minnesota-Federal-Court-Rejects-Insureds-Attempt-to-Cast-Social-Engineering-Fraud-As-Computer-Fraud-Under-Crime-Policy (secondary)
- Perry & Perry Builders, Inc. v. Cowbell Cyber, Inc. and Obsidian Specialty Ins. Co. (W.D. Tex. Mar. 9, 2026). Policy: a $250,000 per-claim Cyber-Crime Loss Limit, with an endorsement applying "regardless of the number of Claims [or] Cyber Crime Incidents". Outcome: summary judgment for the insurers; two wires ($601,866.25 and $272,997.45) to a spoofed steel vendor's account were one capped loss, so only one $250,000 payment was owed. No verification condition was reported. URL: https://www.itpaystobecovered.com/2026/03/texas-federal-court-reinforces-single-limit-for-social-engineering-loss-arising-from-multiple-payments/ (secondary)
- Gore, Kilpatrick & Dambrino, PLLC v. Spinnaker Ins. Co., No. 4:25-cv-107, 2026 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 69567 (N.D. Miss. Mar. 31, 2026). Policy: Social Engineering endorsement requiring an instruction "purporting to be from" a person "who exchanges, or is under contract to exchange, goods or services with the Named Insured for a fee" and "sent by an imposter". Outcome: the court granted the insurer's motion to dismiss because the impersonated person was never an existing client of the firm (the firm reportedly also phoned to confirm before wiring, so confirmation did not rescue coverage). URLs: https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2026/05/01/no-coverage-under-cyber-policy-for-law-firm-defrauded-by-imposter-(insurance-law-alert) and https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/the-illusion-of-cyber-coverage-how-a-court-narrowed-social-engineering-insurance/ (both secondary)
- Office of the Special Deputy Receiver v. Hartford Fire Ins. Co., No. 25-2309 (7th Cir. 2026; Clausen Miller article dated 2026-08-18, filing date not stated). Policy: Financial Institution Bond Rider 17 covers fraudulent instruction sent electronically purportedly by a customer, but excludes "fraudulent instruction sent to OSD through electronic mail" otherwise. Outcome: the Seventh Circuit affirmed dismissal for Hartford on the email exclusion, for about a $4M net loss from a phished CFO account; no callback facts reported. URL: https://www.clausen.com/7th-circuit-sides-with-insurer-bars-coverage-for-hackers-fraud/ (secondary; quoted wording is the article's paraphrase)
- Unnamed bank v. its insurer (court and caption not stated; summary judgment Feb. 11, 2020). Policy: Financial Institution Bond forgery grant requiring a "written, original" transfer request; unauthorized-signature rider; computer-use cover. Outcome: two of three counts dismissed for the insurer (a PDF attached to an email was not a written original); the insurer also argued the bank's two managers skipped callback procedures, but the article does not say the court ruled on that point. URL: https://www.goldbergsegalla.com/news-and-knowledge/case_studies/court-rules-social-engineering-scam-not-covered-under-banks-forgery-and-unauthorized-signatures-policies/ (secondary, insurer counsel's own case study)

## Gaps
- No court decision found (2019-2026) where a claim was denied and the denial was upheld because the insured skipped a callback, dual authorization or verification step. Law-firm commentary (Legal500 / Ward and Smith, Lowenstein "Call Me, Maybe?", 2023-02-27) says such conditions exist ("independently verify" via a method "other than" the one used to make the change) but names no cases.
