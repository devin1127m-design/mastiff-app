# R1 carrier requirement audit: insurtech carriers (Coalition, At-Bay, Cowbell, Corvus)

Researched 2026-10-24. Desk research on public sources only. Not legal or coverage advice.

How to read this file
- PRIMARY = carrier-issued application, policy form, endorsement, or court filing quoting the policy. SECONDARY = carrier web page or blog. THIRD-PARTY = broker or law-firm commentary.
- Quotes marked [text-extracted] were pulled from the PDF's text layer by me and are exact (apostrophes normalised). Quotes marked [page-fetch] came from a web fetch summariser and are short phrases only; re-check before putting them in client-facing copy.
- "Specimen" forms are unsigned samples. Real policies may carry state amendments or negotiated endorsements.

Headline finding for Mastiff
- Callback / out-of-band verification shows up in two places: (a) underwriting questions (a misstatement can support rescission) and (b) a few forms where it is written into the coverage itself. Only Coalition's endorsement CYUSP-50EN-000108-0225-01 turns it into a hard exclusion in the documents I could read. Cowbell, At-Bay and Corvus base forms I read have no callback condition in the insuring agreement text.
- Dual control is asked by At-Bay ($25,000 threshold, two employees) and Coalition (dual control, $25,000 threshold). Cowbell and Corvus ask for separate-channel verification instead.
- Sublimits are low: $250,000 recurs for Coalition FTF, Cowbell social engineering and Cowbell cyber crime. At-Bay advertises up to $500K (conditional) and $1M (higher tier).

Does Named Insured require dual control when transferring funds in excess of $25,000?" with footnote "Dual control for transferring funds refers to a process by which a transfer must be approved by someone other than the initiator of the transfer on a different computer and/or device". Document: "Questions in a Coalition Cyber Policy Application", Coalition Insurance Solutions, Inc. Undated. https://files.thecoalition.com/Coalition-Cyber-Policy-Application-Questions.pdf [text-extracted]
- PRIMARY, secondary-channel (older form). "6 Does Named Insured require a secondary means of communication to validate the authenticity of funds transfers (ACH, wire, etc.) requests before processing a request in excess of $25,000?" Document: Coalition Cyber and Tech E&O renewal application, file stamped "RENEWAL 202012" (Dec 2020). https://files.thecoalition.com/Coalition-Cyber-and-Tech-Errors-Omissions-RENEWAL-Application-202012.pdf [text-extracted]
- PRIMARY, dual control with slightly different footnote. "6 Does the Named Insured require dual control when transferring funds in excess of $25,000? *" with footnote "...approved or confirmed by someone other than the initiator of the transfer." Document: broker-hosted Coalition fillable application, upload path 2022/08, form itself undated. https://www.cresinsurance.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Cyber-Coalition-App-Fillable.pdf [text-extracted]

- Not followed: a fourth hit (proriskgroup.com 2021 Coalition application) redirected to an unrelated domain; I did not follow it.

### Coverage wording and sublimits
- PRIMARY, the callback condition exists as an endorsement. Form "FUNDS TRANSFER LOSS ENDORSEMENT REQUIRING AUTHENTICATION", Form Number CYUSP-50EN-000108-0225-01 (edition Feb 2025), attached to the Coalition Cyber Policy issued to Association of California Water Agencies JPIA, policy period 1 Jul 2025 to 1 Jul 2026 (Arch, Aspen, Chaucer, Fireman's Fund, Fortegra, Vantage quota share). Verbatim: "Funds transfer loss will not include any loss arising out of any fraudulent instruction received by you that is not authenticated by you through a secondary method of authentication to verify the authenticity or validity of such instruction in addition to the original method of authentication undertaken. Such secondary method of authentication must: (i) be different from the original method of authentication undertaken; and (ii) utilize contact information that is not obtained from the fraudulent instruction received by you." https://www.acwajpia.com/wp-content/uploads/25-26-Cyber-Liability-Coalition-policy.pdf (pp. 81-82 of PDF) [text-extracted]
  - Caveat: this is a negotiated endorsement on a $5M public-entity program placed by Aon, so I cannot say it is attached to Coalition small-business policies. It is a published Coalition-owned form number, so it is a real option the carrier uses. Ask the broker whether it is on the client's policy.
- PRIMARY, base FTF definition. Coalition Cyber Policy form SP 14 798 0419 (same ACWA policy). "Funds transfer fraud means a fraudulent instruction transmitted by electronic means, including through social engineering, to you or your financial institution directing you, or the financial institution, to debit an account of the named insured or subsidiary and to transfer, pay, or deliver money or securities from such account, which instruction purports to have been transmitted by an insured and impersonates you or your vendors, business partners, or clients, but was transmitted by someone other than you, and without your knowledge or consent." [text-extracted]
- PRIMARY, insuring agreement. "J. FUNDS TRANSFER FRAUD: We will pay on your behalf direct funds transfer loss that you incur resulting from a funds transfer fraud first discovered by you during the policy period." Loss = "loss of money or securities directly resulting from funds transfer fraud" plus "reasonable and necessary costs, fees, and expenses to respond". Same source. [text-extracted]
- PRIMARY, sublimit and retention in that declaration. "J. FUNDS TRANSFER FRAUD $250,000 $50,000" (limit, retention) against a $5,000,000 aggregate. Large-entity numbers; small-business retentions will be lower. Same source. [text-extracted]
- PRIMARY, Invoice Manipulation (inverse risk: your customers pay a fraudster). Endorsement SP 17 813 0819: "Invoice Manipulation means the release or distribution of any fraudulent invoice or payment instruction to a third party as a direct result of a security failure." Sublimit "$250,000 $50,000" in the ACWA schedule. This does not cover Mastiff's outbound-payment scenario. Same source. [text-extracted]
- PRIMARY, Canada comparator (Lloyd's/Westport/Arch, policy period 26 Aug 2022 to 26 Aug 2023, form CYBCAN 0001 0320). "R. FUNDS TRANSFER FRAUD, PERSONAL FUNDS FRAUD, AND SOCIAL ENGINEERING $250,000 $12,500"; "U. INVOICE MANIPULATION $250,000 $2,500". Insuring agreement: "We will pay funds transfer loss that you incur resulting from a funds transfer fraud or personal funds fraud first discovered by you during the policy period." No callback condition in the text I read. Not a US form. https://www.sonomapines.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Coalition-Lloyd-Sadd-Cyber-Liability..pdf [text-extracted]
- SECONDARY, marketing. "Coverage for financial losses incurred from a fraudulent instruction or social engineering" "that results in a fraudulent transfer of funds." https://www.coalitioninc.com/coverages [page-fetch]. And: "(Sub-limit may apply. Please read the policy for all coverages, terms, exclusions, and conditions.)" https://www.coalitioninc.com/topics/what-is-funds-transfer-fraud, undated, cites 2025 data [page-fetch].
- SECONDARY, retention incentive. Coalition's Active Cyber Policy launch materials say insureds reporting FTF within 72 hours of the transfer get lower FTF retentions (businesswire 20250409047735, April 2025; coalitioninc.com/campaign/active-cyber-policy). Retrieved via search result, page not opened by me; amounts not found. Also 2024 announcement extended the FTF trigger to deepfake or AI-generated instructions (businesswire 20240326463471).
- SECONDARY, crime vs cyber. "only the single highest retention between the two policies will apply" when both respond. https://www.coalitioninc.com/blog/cyber-insurance/funds-transfer-fraud-how-coverage-responds (28 Oct 2024) [page-fetch].

### Claims guidance
- SECONDARY, carrier controls guidance (not a policy condition). Blog, 19 Aug 2021, lists "calling the requesting party on a known good number to confirm the demand", "never use the contact information provided in an email", "a defined, two-party approval process for transfers", "required reviews for payment change details". https://www.coalitioninc.com/blog/insurance/new-claims-report-sees-sharp-increase-in-funds-transfer-fraud-attacks [page-fetch]
- SECONDARY. Controls page: "Coalition recommends implementing a 'dual control' process" including "One administrator or user enters or creates a payment (ACH batch, wire transfer), and a second administrator or user is then required to review the payment and approve/release the transaction"; and "(Note: Use a known-good phone number, not one in the email)". https://www.coalitioninc.com/topics/what-is-funds-transfer-fraud [page-fetch]
- SECONDARY, timing. "Notify Coalition's claims team of the loss as soon as possible, ideally within 72 hours of the transfer." (19 Aug 2021). Clawback post, 10 Jul 2025: "While the first 48 hours often play a crucial role in our ability to claw back funds." https://www.coalitioninc.com/blog/funds-transfer-fraud-3-steps-to-a-successful-clawback [page-fetch]. Claims line shown on site: +1 (833) 866-1337.
- Denials or disputes: none found involving Coalition and a skipped callback.

### Tools
- No vendor-verification or payee-validation tool offered or required. Coalition Control (risk platform) and a vanishing-retention endorsement exist but concern security posture, not payments. https://www.coalitioninc.com/coverages [page-fetch]
- MFA retention reduction endorsement SP 17 814 0819 ("reduction of $10,000" when MFA was enabled) appears in the ACWA policy; unrelated to payment verification. [text-extracted]

## At-Bay

### Application questions
- PRIMARY, dual control. Cyber Insurance Application AB-CYB-APP, edition 06/2026, Financial Fraud section: "11. Does the Applicant have controls in place which require all fund and wire transfers over $25,000 to be authorized and verified by at least two employees prior to execution?" (Yes/No). Next question is phishing training: "12. Does the Applicant conduct computer network security training for all employees (such as training on phishing prevention)?" Applies to the $100M+ revenue Full Application; the Express application for smaller risks was not found. The application ties itself to policy form AB-CYB-001.2 Ed.08/2023. https://www.at-bay.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/AB-CYB-APP-_-Cyber-Insurance-Application-_-Ed.06.2026-.pdf [text-extracted]
- No callback / out-of-band question found on this form (the two-employee "authorized and verified" wording is the only payment control question).

### Coverage wording and sublimits
- PRIMARY, Cyber Insurance Policy AB-CYB-001.2, 08/2023, At-Bay Specialty Insurance Company. Insuring Agreement I.E.1: "We shall pay the Insured Organization for Fraudulent Inducement Loss and Reward Expense Loss incurred by the Insured Organization as a direct result of Fraudulent Inducement Instructions it receives and accepts and which are first discovered during the Policy Period." https://www.at-bay.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Cyber-Insurance-Policy-Form.pdf [text-extracted]
- PRIMARY, definitions. "Fraudulent Inducement Instructions means the misrepresentation of one or more facts by a third-party person or entity via email or other means of electronic communication with the intent of misleading an Insured into transferring Funds or Securities." "Funds or Securities does not include any crypto-currencies or crypto-assets." Same form. [text-extracted]
- PRIMARY, exclusion relevant to forged payment documents. Exclusions applicable to Financial Fraud, item 3 "Forged or Altered Instruments": "resulting directly from forged, altered, or fraudulent negotiable instruments, securities, documents or written instructions or instructions used as source documentation to enter electronic data or send instructions." Check how this reads against fake invoices with changed bank details (my reading: arguable, not tested). Same form. [text-extracted]
- No callback or verification condition precedent appears in the insuring agreement, definitions or financial-fraud exclusions of this form. Sublimits and retentions sit in Declarations Item 6, which a specimen form does not fill in.
- SECONDARY, sublimit tiers. Core tier: "Fraud Defense (activation unlocks up to $500K financial fraud sub-limit)"; Complete tier: "$0 financial fraud retention" and "Up to $1M financial fraud sub-limit". Footnote: "For financial fraud arising from certain causes; see policy for details." and enhancements "subject to qualifying conditions". https://www.at-bay.com/insurance/cyber/ (no date, 2026 footer) [page-fetch]. Higher sublimit linked to Stance MDR for Email in the 5 Feb 2025 press release (businesswire 20250205089539), seen via search only.
- THIRD-PARTY. A Boxpro Insurance article compared five cyber policies including At-Bay on one wire-fraud fact pattern and said four denied; it did not clearly say which. Not relied on. https://www.boxproinsurance.com/resources/the-wire-fraud-your-ria-cyber-policy-wasn-t-built-for

### Claims guidance
- SECONDARY. "Quick Wins to Improve Fraud Defenses" by Adam Tyra, At-Bay CISO for Customers, 12 Nov 2025: banking-change notices should be handled by "mandating confirmation through trusted methods like phone calls or face-to-face meetings"; call "a pre-verified vendor number"; verify "through alternate channels before executing transactions", especially above $10,000. Says average financial fraud loss ($268K) exceeds "the typical maximum cyber insurance coverage of $250K." https://www.at-bay.com/articles/fraud-defense-best-practices/ [page-fetch]
- Denials or disputes tied to a skipped callback: none found for At-Bay.

### Tools
- Stance Fraud Defense: email security for Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace; needs a policy placed through At-Bay Insurance Services, LLC with an "Embedded Security Endorsement"; "It is at the sole discretion of the Named Insured to engage". Description: "stopping social engineering and business email compromise in its tracks". https://www.at-bay.com/stance/fraud-defense/ (no date, 2026 footer) [page-fetch]. It is email filtering, not payee or bank-detail verification, and not a stated coverage requirement.

## Cowbell

### Application questions
- PRIMARY, Prime 100 application, form SP NY 98 02 23 (Spinnaker Insurance Company paper), completed example for a New York village, policy period 1 Jun 2025 to 1 Jun 2026. Section V, "5. Do you want to purchase coverage for Social Engineering Incidents?" and if yes:
  - "5.1 Do You or Your employees verify third-party bank accounts before adding to your accounts payable systems?"
  - "5.2 Do You have an established and documented verification procedure requiring that You or Your employees authenticate all funds transfer requests, whether made to a third-party or otherwise by way of a secondary method of communication from the initial request for funds transfer (as one example, by calling the intended recipient to verify the request at a predetermined phone number if the funds transfer request was made by email)?"
  - "5.3 Do You or Your employees prevent unauthorized employees from initiating wire transfers?"
  https://ryebrook.civicweb.net/document/192938/ [text-extracted]. The Yes/No tick marks did not extract, so I do not know how that applicant answered.
- Same form: "Misrepresentation of any material fact in this Application may be grounds for the rescission of this Policy." (Section VI.)
- No dual-authorization question; 5.3 is the nearest (unauthorised initiators blocked).

### Coverage wording and sublimits
- PRIMARY, Prime 100 specimen policy, declarations SP CW 97 12 25 and Funds Transfer Fraud and Social Engineering endorsement SP CW 49 12 25 (Dec 2025 edition), Spinnaker Insurance Company, marked SPECIMEN. https://home.sayatalabs.com/cnc-wb/get_resource/carriers/SAMPLE_POLICY_FORM/COWBELL/P100 [text-extracted]
  - Social Engineering insuring agreement: "We will pay for Social Engineering Loss resulting directly from a Social Engineering Incident involving an Insured's loss of Money, that is first Discovered during the Policy Period or any Extended Reporting Period, if applicable, and reported in accordance with Condition 14."
  - "Social Engineering Incident means the intentional misleading of an Insured to transfer Money to a person, place or account beyond the Named Insured's control resulting directly from the Named Insured's employee's good faith reliance upon an instruction transmitted electronically, purporting to be from: 1. a Vendor; 2. an employee of the Named Insured; but which contained a Fraudulent Instruction or material misrepresentation and was sent by an imposter."
  - Fraudulent Instruction (SE) includes "instructions produced or manipulated by deep fakes, synthetic media, or other artificial intelligence technologies".
  - FTF insuring agreement covers fraudulent entry or change of data in a Computer System by someone without authorization, or a Fraudulent Instruction to a financial institution to debit the Transfer Account "without Your knowledge or consent".
  - Exclusion for both: loss from "A fraudulent, dishonest or criminal act by any Employee or authorized representative of the Named Insured". "Money does not include digital currency".
  - Sublimits and deductibles are separate lines (FTF limit, SE limit, Invoice Manipulation limit) and are blank in the specimen. Sublimits are "part of, not in addition to" the policy aggregate.
  - **No verification or callback condition appears in this endorsement.** I searched the full specimen for verif/authenticat/predetermined and found none attached to SE. The "documented verification procedure" requirement is stated in marketing (below) but I could not locate it in policy text. Treat it as an underwriting representation until the issued policy is read.
- SECONDARY, marketing. Prime 100 overview, (c)2024: "A documented verification procedure requirement needs to have been completed in order to be provided coverage." Description reads "an instruction transmitted via mail by an imposter". https://cowbell.insure/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/CB-Prime100-Overview.pdf [text-extracted]. Product page repeats it with "must be completed for coverage to be provided" and "limits of up to $3M" (not clear which coverage). https://cowbell.insure/prime-100-pro/ [page-fetch]
- SECONDARY, 2020 launch: social engineering as an endorsement "with a limit up to $250,000", "deductible as low as $10,000" (15 Apr 2020). https://cowbell.insure/?p=3626 [page-fetch]. Rye Brook application above shows Social Engineering limit $250,000 and deductible $10,000 in 2025.
- PRIMARY (court filing), Cowbell/Obsidian Specialty Insurance Company paper, a different Cowbell form: "Social Engineering Attack" means "the manipulation or deception, by an unauthorized third party, of a director, officer, ... or employee ... who is authorized to request or make payments on behalf of the Insured Organization. The manipulation or deception must be accomplished by the transmission of fraudulent communications by the unauthorized third party..." Cyber Crime Loss sublimit "$250,000" "Each Claim Limit of Liability". No verification condition in the quoted definition. Defendants' Motion for Summary Judgment, Perry & Perry Builders v. Cowbell Cyber & Obsidian, No. 6:25-cv-00106-LS (W.D. Tex.), filed 7 Jul 2025. https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Cowbell-MSJ-Cyber-Policy-.pdf [text-extracted]

### Claims guidance
- PRIMARY (dispute, not a denial for skipped callback). Perry & Perry: fraudster posing as steel vendor Alamo asked to switch from checks to ACH; office manager sent two ACH payments a minute apart ($272,997.45 and about $601,886.25) on 20 Dec 2023. Obsidian "acknowledged that the social engineering attack was a covered Cyber Crime Incident and paid Perry & Perry the $250,000 Cyber Crime Loss sublimit"; fight was over a second $250,000. Court (W.D. Tex., 9 Mar 2026, per law-firm blog) held one limit applies. Lesson for Mastiff: the loss was roughly 3.5x the sublimit, so the sublimit, not verification, drove the shortfall. https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/cyber-fraud-limits-read-the-cyber-policy-carefully-before-the-cyber-thieves-read-your-email/ [THIRD-PARTY, page-fetch]. The blog says the office manager phoned the number given in the fraudulent email; the MSJ text I read does not mention a call, so that detail is unconfirmed.
- SECONDARY, Cowbell claim case study (c) 2022, PDF created 21 Apr 2023: supplier "switching to bank ABC"; policyholder checked contact details and branding, updated bank info and paid, then "called the supplier to confirm" and learned the details had not changed. Cowbell contacted within the hour; policyholder "recover[ed] 80% of the fraudulently transferred funds" and "Cowbell made the policyholder whole." Note the call came AFTER payment, so this does not show a pre-payment callback was required. https://cowbell.insure/uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2022/12/Cowbell-Claim-Case-Study-Social-Engineering-.pdf [text-extracted]
- No Cowbell denial citing a skipped verification procedure found.

### Tools
- No vendor-verification tool. Cowbell Factors, Insights, Connectors, Resiliency Services and Wizer-based awareness training (free seats first policy year) are posture tools. Cowbell Prime 100 overview, (c)2024 [text-extracted].

## Corvus (Travelers-owned)

### Application questions
- PRIMARY, Smart Cyber Insurance Application, Version 3.2, September 2024. "19. Prior to executing an electronic payment, do you verify the validity of the funds transfer request or payment change request, with the requestor, via a separate means of communication prior to transferring funds or making payment changes?" (Yes/No; superscript 3 appears keyed to the glossary). Glossary: "Out-of-Band Authentication (OOBA) Secondary verification method with the requester of a funds transfer through a communication channel separate from the original request. An example of this would be calling a known and trusted phone number to confirm a change in payment instructions sent via email from a vendor." https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/Corvus-Smart-Cyber-Application.pdf/3175bed06e3c11f0b1c796ac6c0501a2 [text-extracted]
- No dual-authorization or payment-limit question found on this form (only question 19 touches payments; 18 is annual security or phishing training).

### Coverage wording and sublimits
- PRIMARY, Corvus Policy for Smart Cyber Insurance, specimen marked "SPECIMEN CB-101-001", 30 pages, undated in the text. https://www.corvusinsurance.com/broker-hub/broker/corvus-policy-for-smart-cyber-insurance.pdf [text-extracted]
  - Insuring Agreement E: "Social Engineering and Cyber Crime Coverage: Financial Fraud Loss, Telecommunications Fraud Loss, Phishing Attack Loss, theft of Funds Held in Escrow, or theft of Personal Funds incurred directly as a result of Financial Fraud, Telecommunications Fraud, or Phishing Attack."
  - "Financial Fraud ... b. An intentional, unauthorized and fraudulent written, electronic or telephonic instruction transmitted to a financial institution by an Executive or Employee as a result of that Executive or Employee receiving intentional, misleading or deceptive telephonic or electronic communications from a Third Party falsely purporting to be the Insured or the Insured's client, vendor, Executive or Employee, and which directs the financial institution to debit the Insured's account and to transfer, pay or deliver money or securities from the Insured's account".
  - "Financial Fraud Loss does not include any amounts reimbursed to the Insured by any financial institution".
  - **No callback, verification or authentication condition in the insuring agreement or definitions.** Grep for verif/authenticat/callback returned only an MFA mention and the "social engineering techniques" wording inside the Security Breach definition.
  - Possible conflict to ask about: Exclusion 13 "Loss of Funds" reads "c. The value of electronic funds, money, securities or wire transfer" with no carve-back in the text I read. I did not review endorsements, which may restore it.
  - Wording note (my reading): Financial Fraud limb (b) turns on an instruction "transmitted to a financial institution", so a mailed cheque to a fake payee may not fit. Confirm with the broker.
  - Sublimits and retentions are in the declarations and not shown. Invoice Manipulation is not in this base form text (the word "Invoice" appears only in unrelated clauses); a Corvus broker blog says it is a "non-standard agreement".
- SECONDARY, limits history. Business Insurance, 4 Oct 2019: Corvus offered "$1 million in limits" to organizations Corvus Scan rates as having "taken the most effective steps to reduce the risk of a social engineering attack"; prior default was $250,000. Controls were not listed. https://www.businessinsurance.com/article/20191004/NEWS06/912331024/MGU-Corvus-raises-cybercrime-coverage-limits [page-fetch]. Dated; current sublimits not found.
- SECONDARY, Corvus broker blog (published Dec 2020, updated 2 Dec 2021) on what to check: sublimit, callback requirements and voluntary parting wording. https://www.corvusinsurance.com/broker/cyber-coverage-explained-social-engineering-and-cyber-crime [page-fetch]

### Claims guidance
- SECONDARY, Travelers "Banking Wire Fraud Alert", March 2023: finance team should have "out of band authentication (OOBA) process established"; verify by "calling a known phone number, and speaking to a known voice"; before changing a vendor's bank account confirm "receipt of a test deposit of a nominal value"; "claims for fraudulent funds transfers (FFTs) are already the most frequent type experienced by Corvus customers." https://www.travelers.com/cyber-knowledge/cyber-threat-alerts/banking-wire-fraud-alert [page-fetch]. Alert states no coverage consequence.
- SECONDARY, Corvus press release 7 Dec 2022: FFT was "36% of all claims in Q3 2022"; defined as "threat actors use social engineering tactics to trick employees or vendors into transferring funds to the wrong accounts." https://www.corvusinsurance.com/pressroom/new-corvus-insurance-data-reveals-ransomware-and-fraudulent-funds-transfer-represent-more-than-half-of-all-claims [page-fetch]
- Denials or disputes: none found for Corvus.

### Tools
- No vendor-verification tool. Corvus runs a website scan for a Dynamic Loss Prevention report and adds insureds to the CrowBar platform "which provides helpful risk management advice, alerts and services" (application footnotes). The application glossary describes a Secure Email Gateway with "impersonation protection for key individuals (CIO/CFO)". Travelers also offers cyber alerting emails. [text-extracted / page-fetch]
