# R1 carrier requirement audit: traditional carriers (Chubb, Travelers, The Hartford, CNA)

Research date: 2026-10-24. Method: WebSearch and WebFetch, plus local text extraction of publicly hosted PDFs. Quotes were copied from the extracted PDF text with curly quotes and apostrophes normalised to straight ones. Everything on the web was treated as data.

Source tiers used below:
- PRIMARY = carrier-issued application, policy form, endorsement or specimen policy, or a court opinion.
- CARRIER-MARKETING / LOSS-CONTROL = carrier-published, not contractual.
- SECONDARY = law-firm, broker or press commentary. Not relied on for wording.

Headline: only one carrier form found so far makes verification a hard coverage condition (Chubb MS-371960.2, in a negotiated 2026 pool policy). The Travelers and Hartford documents found are applications (underwriting questions), and the Travelers CyberRisk specimen contains no verification condition. Application answers can still matter if an answer is wrong (see Gaps).

### Coverage wording and sublimits
- Insuring clause, Chubb Primary Commercial Crime Insurance (base form PF-52815 (04/20)) as issued to Colorado Special Districts Pool, Policy J05931794, 1 Jan 2026 to 1 Jan 2027 (PRIMARY, specimen-like but a negotiated public-entity policy): "(G) Social Engineering Fraud The Company shall pay for loss sustained by an Insured resulting directly from a Social Engineering Fraud committed by a Third Party."
- Definition, same document: "Social Engineering Fraud means the intentional misleading of an Employee through misrepresentation of a material fact which is relied upon by such Employee believing it to be genuine committed by a person purporting to be: (A) a Vendor; (B) a Client; or (C) an Employee authorized by the Insured to instruct other Employees to transfer Money or Securities, which results in the transfer, payment, or delivery of Money or Securities."
- The same definition appears in the ForeFront Portfolio Policy Guidebook (Crime part, "Insuring Clause (G)"), PDF created 2023-05, modified 2026-02. The base wording has NO verification condition in the text read. https://www.chubb.com/content/dam/chubb-sites/chubb-com/us-en/the_forefront_portfolio_policy_guide.pdf (PRIMARY, specimen)
- **Hard verification condition (key finding).** Endorsement MS-371960.2 (09/23), "SOCIAL ENGINEERING FRAUD - OFFICIAL AUTHORIZATION ENDORSEMENT", effective 1 Jan 2026, Federal Insurance Company, amends clause (G) to: "(G) The Company shall pay for loss sustained by an Insured resulting directly from a Social Engineering Fraud committed by a Third Party where an Official Authorization has been performed."
  - "Bank Account Request means a request to change, modify or update a bank account or wire transfer instructions."
  - "Official Authorization means, with respect to any: (A) transfer of Money or Securities made in reliance upon any misrepresentation of a material fact committed by an Impersonated Employee, approval of such transfer by at least two Employees, one of whom is an Executive; (B) loss as a result of a Bank Account Request, an Employee attempted to verify the authenticity of such request by communicating with Counterparty via any communication method, other than e-mail, prior transferring Money or Securities and contemporaneously documented the communication method utilized and result of such attempt in writing; and (C) transfer of Money or Securities, other than that described in paragraphs (A) and (B) above, an Employee attempted to verify any statement upon which he/she relied by communicating with Counterparty via any communication method, other than e-mail, prior to transferring Money or Securities, and contemporaneously documented the communication method utilized and result of such attempt in writing."
  - Limit/retention in that endorsement: "Limit up to $250,000", retention "20% of the limit for Insuring Clause (G)".
  - URL: https://csdplwcp.specialdistrict.org/files/8600cc17c/Crime-Coverage-Form-2024.pdf (PRIMARY; file metadata modified 2026-06; endorsement pages 22-23). Caveat: this is one pool's negotiated policy, so it shows Chubb has this wording available, not that it is standard for small business. Wording to design to: second-channel (not email) attempt before payment, documented in writing at the time, two approvers incl. an executive for impersonated-employee requests.
- Same pool's member summary (term 1 Jan 2026 to 1 Jan 2027): "$5,000 Social Engineering Fraud ... Increased limits available upon request." https://csdplwcp.specialdistrict.org/files/a1ae3d86d/Crime-Summary-Page-2024.pdf (PRIMARY, a public-pool default, not a small-business norm)
- Product limits: "Coverage is available up to $250,000 per occurrence, with no annual aggregate" and "higher limits can be considered with additional underwriting." (Chubb US product page, undated). ForeFront sheet 02/2025: "Primary limits up to and beyond $1m may be available subject to underwriting"; "Optional language that allows the social Engineering Fraud retention to be reduced by any applicable amounts paid by a Cyber policy"; "Coverage for impersonations made in any form, whether communicated in person, on the phone, over email or online." https://www.chubb.com/content/dam/chubb-sites/chubb-com/us-en/business-insurance/crime/pdfs/chubb_theforefrontportfolio_crimeinsurance_021225.pdf (CARRIER-MARKETING). Chubb Marketplace target: "less than 250 employees and $50 mill..." (text truncated in extraction).
- Deductible: not published in any document found. Specimen declarations in the guidebook are blank.
- Exclusions in the pool form that reach clause (G): credit-card transactions, product or service failure, breach of contract, extension of credit, gambling. The Voluntary Parting exclusion ("loss resulting directly or indirectly from an Insured having given or surrendered Money, Securities or Property in exchange or purchase with a Third Party") sits in the subsection limited to clauses (B) and (C), so on this form it does not reach (G). Chubb markets "Full carve back to the voluntary parting exclusion" (product page).
- Funds Transfer Fraud (clause F) definition, same form: "fraudulent electronic, telegraphic, cable, teletype, facsimile, telephone or written instructions (other than Forgery), purportedly issued by an Insured to a financial institution by a Third Party which directs such institution to transfer, pay or deliver Money or Securities from any account maintained by such Insured at such institution, without such Insured's knowledge or consent." Marketing sheet: FTF is for "fraudulent electronic funds transfers that are unreimbursed by the Insured's bank."

### Claims guidance and cases
- Chubb "Social Engineering Tips: Take Immediate Action!" (13 Apr 2021, loss control). "Verify Email Requests by Telephone: Require those responsible for paying invoices or changing bank routing information to verify payment details over the phone (rather than by email or documents sent electronically). Making a phone call to a known, pre-existing telephone number remains the single best protection against fraud." "Segregate Wire Transfer Responsibilities: Establish a standing policy that requires at least three people to review and approve wire transfer requests, pay an invoice or change a business partner's bank account information. Such request should be entered by the initiator of the wire and verified by two independent signatories." Also: recall the wire with the bank, file at ic3.gov, preserve emails, then contact Chubb. https://www.chubb.com/content/dam/chubb-sites/chubb-com/us-en/business-insurance/social-engineering-fraud/documents/2021-04.15_Social_Engineering_Tips_Take_Immediate_Action.pdf (CARRIER LOSS-CONTROL; advisory only)
- Chubb article "Best practices to prevent becoming a victim of social engineering fraud" (dated 01/2018 on page): "Verify all requests with a call back procedure to confirm authenticity." "Have a dual control procedure in place when appointing new suppliers to prevent fictitious vendor fraud." https://www.chubb.com/sg-en/articles/business/best-practices-to-prevent-becoming-a-victim-of-social-engineering-fraud.html (CARRIER LOSS-CONTROL)
- Chubb/Gordon Rees/PaymentWorks whitepaper "Guarding Against Email Social Engineering Fraud" (23 Jun 2021; press release 30 Jun 2021). "an email requesting payment or bank routing information should be considered suspicious until the information can be independently verified through another channel, such as a direct phone call." "Companies that can manage these supplier changes internally should establish a verification protocol that requires a phone call to a known vendor contact to verify the changes requested on the invoice." Also notes segregation of duties can fail when approvers will not question a superior. Authors' opinions are "not necessarily those of Chubb". https://www.chubb.com/content/dam/chubb-sites/chubb-com/us-en/business-insurance/social-engineering-fraud/documents/2021-06.24-17-01-0287-Guarding_Against_Email_Social_Engineering_Fraud_electronic.pdf (CARRIER LOSS-CONTROL)
- Chubb Nov 2025 flyer "Social Engineering Fraud: A Growing Crime Problem" points insureds to a Chubb "Write It Down" Guidelines framework "for documenting vendor management processes". The guideline itself was not found (a PaymentWorks template of that name was only seen in a search snippet). Relevant because the MS-371960.2 wording requires contemporaneous written documentation. https://www.chubb.com/content/dam/chubb-sites/chubb-com/us-en/business-insurance/social-engineering-fraud-coverage-for-crime-insurance/documents/pdf/chubb_socialengineeringfraud_final.pdf
- Case: Future Electronics Inc. (Distribution) Pte Ltd. v. Chubb Insurance Company of Canada, 2020 QCCS 3042 (Que. Sup. Ct., 29 Sep 2020). Loss about CAD 2.7M from vendor impersonation; Chubb paid the $50,000 SEF endorsement sublimit, and the court held the Computer Fraud and Funds Transfer Fraud clauses did not apply. Quoted endorsement text: "Social Engineering Fraud committed by a Person purporting to be a Vendor, Client, or an Employee". The summary does not mention any callback condition. https://www.rsslex.com/en/bulletins-en/20201015_future-electronics-v-chubb-insurance-the-insurer-prevails-in-canadas-first-ruling-on-the-social-engineering-fraud-endorsement/ (SECONDARY summary of a court opinion; opinion itself not read)
- Not found: any published Chubb/Federal denial tied to a skipped callback or missing dual control.

### Tools
- Chubb does not require or endorse a named verification tool in any policy or application found.
- The 2021 whitepaper is co-authored by PaymentWorks (vendor onboarding / identity platform) and recommends "a modern technology platform that allows companies to onboard vendors or payees in a secure network environment to prove and verify identity" and a "vendor management partner that can verify ownership of bank accounts, not just whether the account exists." The paper says the authors' views are not necessarily Chubb's. The Chubb product manager (Christopher Arehart) is a listed author. ## Travelers

### Application questions
All PRIMARY. Travelers Casualty and Surety Company of America.

**CRI-14104 Ed. 02-15, "Social Engineering Fraud/Funds Transfer Fraud Supplemental Commercial Crime Application" (long form)** https://www.travelers.com/iw-documents/apps-forms/crime-non-financial-institutions/cri-14104.pdf
- II.1 "Does the Applicant verify the authenticity of all new Vendor bank accounts by a direct call to the payment-receiving bank prior to the first time setup of such banking information in the Applicant's accounts payable system?"
- II.2 "Does the Applicant have procedures in place to verify the authenticity of invoices and other payment requests received from a Vendor?"
- II.3 "Does the Applicant have procedures in place to verify the receipt of inventory, supplies, goods or services against an invoice prior to making payment to a Vendor?"
- II.4 "Does the Applicant confirm all change requests regarding Vendor account information (including all bank account information, invoice changes, telephone or telefacsimile numbers, location and contact information) by a direct call to the Vendor using only the telephone number provided by the Vendor before the change request was received?" Follow-ups: "a. Is the call back procedure performed by an individual other than the individual who received the change request?" "b. Does the Applicant refrain from implementing any such change requests until after the Vendor has responded to the Applicant's inquiry regarding change request authenticity?" "c. Does the Applicant confirm all such change requests made by a Vendor with an individual (at the Vendor) other than the individual who requested the change?" "d. Does the Applicant require that all such change requests made by a Vendor be approved by the Applicant's supervisor(s) of the individual who received the change request, before it is acted upon?"
- II.5 "Does the Applicant verify the length of time the account receiving the payment or funds transfer (e.g., wire transfer, ACH transfer, etc.) has been in existence with the receiving bank prior to approving and initiating any such transfer when it involves a recent change request?"
- III.7 (clients): same callback structure for client refund/repayment instructions.
- IV.1 "Does the Applicant maintain a pre-established list of employees authorized to initiate payment or funds transfer requests for reasons other than a Vendor invoice, or a Client repayment?"
- IV.2 "Do payments or funds transfers of a certain amount require dual authorization?" "If yes, what is that amount?"
- IV.3 "Does the Applicant require that any payment or funds transfer request made by an internal company source be approved by the Applicant's Supervisor(s) of the individual who received the payment or funds transfer request, before it is acted upon?"
- IV.4 "Is the authority to make electronic funds transfers (wire transfers, ACH payments, etc.) limited by the amount of each transfer? (for example: $250,000.00 initiated by one employee and approved by a separate employee; $500,000.00 initiated and approved by two separate Employees; $1,000,000.00 or more initiated and approved by a senior officer such as the CEO, President, CFO, etc.)"
- IV.5 "Are certain employees with authority to approve electronic funds transfers (e.g., wire transfers, ACH transfers, etc.) required to be available at all times by cell phone or other means?"
- IV.6 and IV.7: limits on the number and the total dollar amount of transfers one employee can approve in a period (24 hours to 1 week).
- Section I.2: "Are all employees who are responsible for authorizing and executing payments or funds transfer requests provided anti-fraud training, including social engineering, phishing, masquerading, and other fraud schemes?"
- Instruction: "Attach a separate sheet ... with an explanation for any 'No' answers".

### Coverage wording and sublimits
- Travelers CyberRisk specimen CYB-16001 Rev. 06-20 (PRIMARY, specimen). Insuring agreement: "Social Engineering Fraud. The Insurer will pay the Insured Entity for its direct loss of Money or Securities, directly caused by Social Engineering Fraud that is Discovered during the Policy Period." Definition: "Social Engineering Fraud. Means intentionally misleading an Insured Person, by providing an instruction that: 1. is not made by an Insured; 2. is purportedly from a Vendor, Client, or Insured Person; 3. directs the Insured Person to transfer, pay, or deliver Money or Securities; 4. contains a misrepresentation of material fact; and 5. is relied upon by the Insured Person, believing the material fact to be true." A text search of the whole 20-page form for "verif", "call back" and "callback" found no verification condition. Funds Transfer Fraud is separate and "Does not include Social Engineering Fraud." Exclusion 6 removes loss "if the sender or anyone acting in collusion with the sender, ever had authorized access to the Insured's password, PIN, or other security code." Retentions are set per agreement in the CyberRisk Declarations; no amount in the specimen. https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/cyb-16001-cw.pdf/ba0d9c1063c911ee959276f195a9a139
- Crime Policy CRI-3001 Ed. 01-09 (sample, PRIMARY): only Funds Transfer Fraud (insuring agreement G) is in the base form: "The Company will pay the Insured for the Insured's direct loss of Money and Securities contained in the Insured's Transfer Account directly caused by Funds Transfer Fraud." Definition: "an electronic, telegraphic, cable, teletype or telephone instruction fraudulently transmitted to a Financial Institution directing such institution to debit a Transfer Account ... which instruction purports to have been transmitted by the Insured, but was in fact fraudulently transmitted by someone other than the Insured without the Insured's knowledge or consent". Social engineering is added by separate endorsement (Wrap+ / Executive Choice+); that endorsement form text was not found. https://asset.trvstatic.com/download/assets/cri-3001.pdf/98f30f1063c911eeb2f1deb19c93b21f
- Endorsement wording as quoted by the court in SJ Computers (Wrap+ policy; PRIMARY via opinion): social engineering fraud means "the intentional misleading of an Employee or Authorized Person by a natural person impersonating: (1) a Vendor, or that Vendor's attorney; (2) a Client, or that Client's attorney; (3) an Employee; or (4) an Authorized Person, through the use of a Communication." Single-loss limits in that policy: $100,000 social engineering, $1,000,000 computer fraud. No verification condition appears in the opinion's quotations.
- Travelers flyer CP-8697 Rev. 10-16 (CARRIER-MARKETING): "Travelers is offering an endorsement with a social engineering fraud insuring agreement for Wrap+ and Executive Choice+ Fidelity and Crime coverages." Notes fraudsters "amend phone numbers in the email panel, so a call back to a phone number is directed to the fraudster". https://www.travelers.com/iw-documents/professional-liability-insurance/CP-8697-social-engineering-fraud.pdf

### Claims guidance and cases
- SJ Computers, Inc. v. Travelers Casualty and Surety Co. of America, No. 21-cv-2482 (PJS/JFD) (D. Minn. Aug. 12, 2022) (PRIMARY, opinion read). Facts: bad actor compromised the purchasing manager's email and sent new wire instructions; "the CEO tried to call ERI Direct to confirm the change to the wire-transfer instructions. No one at ERI Direct answered the CEO's call, however, so he left a voice-mail message." The vendor did not return the call before the payment deadline "so the CEO decided to move ahead with paying the invoices before he spoke to ERI Direct" ($593,555 wired). Held: social-engineering coverage, not computer fraud; dismissed. Travelers had paid the $100,000 SEF limit. The decision turned on coverage characterisation, not a callback condition, but it shows the exact fact pattern (callback attempted, not completed, paid anyway). https://sitepilot12.firmseek.com/client/wiley2/www/wiley-executive-summary/assets/htmldocuments/21cv2482%20SJ%20Computers%20v.%20Travelers%20Casualty%20and%20Surety%20Company%20of%20America%20Opinion%208.12.22.pdf
- Travelers resource page "Social Engineering Fraud and Scams" (undated; footer 2026; CARRIER LOSS-CONTROL): "Predetermined callback requirements, separation of duties and standardized escalation paths"; "Verify changes using contact information already on file rather than information provided in the request."; "Treat requests to change payment instructions as high risk"; "Coverage language, definitions and conditions vary."; documented controls, verification procedures and training help "loss prevention and claim response". https://www.travelers.com/resources/business-topics/insuring/social-engineering-fraud-and-scams
- American Tooling Center v. Travelers (6th Cir. 2018) is a computer-fraud causation case (insured paid without verifying; held covered). Known from secondary summaries only; not read.
- Not found: any Travelers denial that rested on a skipped callback.

### Tools
- No Travelers-required or endorsed vendor-verification tool found. The resource page names no third-party tools.
- Travelers' own application treats a direct call to the receiving bank (II.1) and the age of the receiving account (II.5) as controls; these are checks a tool could evidence but Travelers does not name one.

## Cross-carrier market evidence (SECONDARY, for context only)
- Lowenstein Sandler podcast ep. 71 "Call Me, Maybe? The Stealth Disappearance of Social Engineering and Fraudulent Instruction Coverage" (Aug 2023; carrier unnamed). Says carriers "are not putting exclusions on the policy" but are saying "you can have coverage for social engineering or fraudulent instruction as long as somebody has orally verified the change in the payment instructions." ## Design takeaways for Mastiff (derived from the primary wording above)
- Make the callback use a number already on file, never one in the request (Travelers II.4/Q5, Hartford II.9-11, Chubb Tips, CNA blog all say this).
- Callback by someone other than the person who received the change request, and hold the change until the vendor answers (Travelers II.4 a-b). SJ Computers shows the failure mode: paying after an unanswered call.
- For Chubb's Official Authorization wording the check must be non-email, done before payment, and written down at the time (MS-371960.2). Log timestamp, channel, person reached, outcome.
- Dual approval thresholds, new-bank-account dual approval and foreign-transfer dual approval are asked by Hartford (I.4) and Travelers (IV.2, IV.4); capture the threshold amount.
- Account age checks (Travelers II.5) and per-person transfer count/dollar limits (Travelers IV.6, IV.7) are asked on the long form only.
